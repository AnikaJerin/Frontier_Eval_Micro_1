# Why encoded weather is challenging

METAR, TAF and SYNOP messages compress several different fields into short codes. A practical decoder must preserve sign digits, units, missing values, time periods and the relevant section or field position. For example, +RA and -RA encode different precipitation intensities; a TAF TEMPO group describes an alternative temporary condition, while an FM group starts a replacement prevailing period. These are examples of domain complexity, not claims that this submission tested every code or identified every model failure.

Four plausible sources of difficulty are compact mixed letter/number groups, interactions across changing forecast periods, high information density, and national or regional reporting differences. They are **hypotheses** about why such work can be hard. The saved responses show incorrect decoded fields, but they do not reveal tokenization, training data or internal reasoning. They do not establish that token boundaries caused the errors, that models lack particular training, or that the errors are universal.

The included evidence supports two narrow observations: GPT-5.4 repeatedly returned incorrect temperature signs in selected SYNOP cases, and in one TAF case it misidentified which layer was the prevailing ceiling. METAR runway-visual-range remarks, gust parsing, +RA/-RA interpretation and many national variants are not directly evaluated in the five-task package. GPT-6 Astra and Muse Wonder passed the included trials under their own settings; the results do not imply all AI systems fail these codes. Each trial's complete answer and its grading are in `results/`, with human-readable examples in EVIDENCE.md.
